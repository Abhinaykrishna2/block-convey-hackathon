1. Purpose and Scope

<!-- evidence:8eb06dc7ed94163675077390 source:word/document.xml#p00021 -->
This Risk Management Policy establishes a consistent framework for identifying, assessing, treating, and monitoring risks that could affect the company's objectives, operations, customers, employees, and legal or contractual obligations. It is designed to be proportionate to the size and stage of the organization rather than modeled on enterprise-scale risk frameworks.

<!-- evidence:7b0785679ee93bfb89da2605 source:word/document.xml#p00022 -->
This policy applies to all business functions, systems, personnel, and third parties engaged by the company, including contractors and vendors with access to company or customer data. It works alongside the company's Information Security Policy, HR Policy, and Incident Response procedures, and provides the framework those policies rely on for identifying and prioritizing risk.

<!-- evidence:dc5c45e4841e92a65a864f80 source:word/document.xml#p00024 -->
2. Roles and Responsibilities

<!-- evidence:6c1743bc9c355f95b016867c source:word/document.xml#p00025 -->
The Risk Owner, typically a founder or a designated Compliance/Risk lead, holds overall responsibility for maintaining the risk management program, coordinating risk assessments, and ensuring the risk register stays current. This role reports significant risks to company leadership.

<!-- evidence:117c7962ead0d9e47bf62b7a source:word/document.xml#p00026 -->
Function and department leads are responsible for identifying risks within their area of operation, such as engineering, sales, finance, or people operations, and for implementing agreed treatment actions within the timelines set during risk assessment.

<!-- evidence:b8be01b57c1b10613e8c61a5 source:word/document.xml#p00027 -->
Company leadership reviews the top risks on a periodic basis and approves acceptance of any risk that exceeds the thresholds defined in this policy. Where the company has a board or investor oversight, a summary of key risks may be shared as part of regular governance updates.

<!-- evidence:e6ab024f2328901fc7a0f073 source:word/document.xml#p00029 -->
3. Risk Management Framework and Methodology

<!-- evidence:869f5fa272de83c0332ef8da source:word/document.xml#p00030 -->
The company uses a qualitative risk assessment approach based on likelihood and impact, rather than complex quantitative modeling, to keep the process practical and usable at the company's current stage. Each identified risk is scored using a simple 1 to 5 scale for both likelihood and impact, and the resulting score determines a risk level of Low, Medium, High, or Critical.

<!-- evidence:07c09013f23c6fb2a68bda8c source:word/document.xml#p00031 -->
Risk assessments are conducted at onboarding of new systems or vendors, prior to major product launches, following security or operational incidents, and as part of the periodic review described later in this policy.

<!-- evidence:ae8732dc351ff6c0818d5fb4 source:word/document.xml#p00033 -->
4. Risk Identification

<!-- evidence:886259b0eddeb4b283ae04e2 source:word/document.xml#p00034 -->
Risks are identified across the following categories: operational (people, process, and internal control risks), information security and cyber (data breaches, system compromise, unauthorized access), third-party and vendor (dependency on external providers and their security posture), legal and regulatory (contractual obligations, data protection law, industry-specific compliance requirements)

<!-- evidence:948c79c28beaa70f0706022c source:word/document.xml#p00035 -->
Risks are surfaced through multiple channels, including internal audits and reviews, incident reports, employee and customer feedback, vendor security assessments, and monitoring of relevant regulatory or industry developments. Any employee may raise a potential risk to their function lead or the Risk Owner at any time.

<!-- evidence:c3bf06eb7b12e29234cebffa source:word/document.xml#p00037 -->
5. Risk Assessment and Scoring

<!-- evidence:ca060182b55f56e5d886df7e source:word/document.xml#p00038 -->
Likelihood is rated on a scale of 1 (rare) to 5 (almost certain), and impact is rated on a scale of 1 (negligible) to 5 (severe), considering financial, operational, legal, and reputational consequences. The risk score is calculated as likelihood multiplied by impact, producing a score between 1 and 25.

<!-- evidence:01fdc43cbec1194496437dfb source:word/document.xml#p00039 -->
Resulting scores are categorized as Low (1 to 5), Medium (6 to 10), High (11 to 15), or Critical (16 to 25). Risk assessments are carried out jointly by the relevant function lead and the Risk Owner, and are documented in the risk register described in Section 7.

<!-- evidence:907625a6275bc2ac100cc903 source:word/document.xml#p00041 -->
6. Risk Treatment

<!-- evidence:73e0f14311b4058f91f99c2a source:word/document.xml#p00042 -->
For each identified risk, the company selects one of four treatment approaches: accept the risk where it falls within acceptable tolerance and the cost of further mitigation is disproportionate; mitigate the risk by implementing controls that reduce likelihood or impact; transfer the risk through mechanisms such as insurance or contractual risk allocation to a third party; or avoid the risk by discontinuing or not proceeding with the activity that creates it.

<!-- evidence:7cee694049890a0002e6f4d8 source:word/document.xml#p00043 -->
Every treatment decision, other than acceptance of Low-scored risks, is documented with a named owner, the specific actions to be taken, and a target completion date. Treatment plans for High and Critical risks require sign-off from company leadership before the risk can be formally accepted or closed.

<!-- evidence:be317cdb2fd01b75c02e2249 source:word/document.xml#p00045 -->
7. Risk Register

<!-- evidence:82bb50ee8661618223851a9c source:word/document.xml#p00046 -->
The risk register is the central record of the company's risk management activity and is maintained by the Risk Owner. Each entry includes a unique risk ID, a description of the risk, its category, likelihood and impact scores, the resulting risk level, the assigned owner, the treatment plan, current status, and the date of next review.

<!-- evidence:aa29e6445c25f5c1d5e72006 source:word/document.xml#p00047 -->
The risk register is reviewed and updated at least annually, and immediately following any significant incident, new system or vendor onboarding, or material change to the business. It serves as supporting evidence for customer, auditor, and investor due diligence requests.

<!-- evidence:5ba9ffc309a3f45462e10eb5 source:word/document.xml#p00049 -->
8. Third-Party and Vendor Risk Management

<!-- evidence:5d49d2c18fdd76cf1277d80f source:word/document.xml#p00050 -->
Before onboarding a critical vendor, defined as one with access to customer data, production systems, or core business infrastructure, the company conducts due diligence appropriate to that vendor's risk tier. This includes review of the vendor's security posture through questionnaires, available certifications such as SOC 2 or ISO 27001, and contractual commitments on data protection and confidentiality.

<!-- evidence:4cabf6bbcb32fcdd77d9e36a source:word/document.xml#p00051 -->
Vendors are tiered based on the sensitivity of data they access and their criticality to operations, with higher-tier vendors subject to more frequent review. Contracts with critical vendors include provisions for breach notification, audit rights where feasible, and data return or destruction obligations at the end of the relationship.

<!-- evidence:34949935905eb72f2d614620 source:word/document.xml#p00053 -->
9. Risk Escalation

<!-- evidence:ffe40f9dcaf596de84ce8850 source:word/document.xml#p00054 -->
Risks scored as High or Critical are escalated to company leadership within five business days of identification. Any risk that exceeds the company's defined risk appetite requires explicit leadership sign-off before it can be accepted rather than mitigated or transferred.

<!-- evidence:13e226f118e54a16d6d57739 source:word/document.xml#p00055 -->
Where an unmitigated or newly realized risk results in an actual incident, such as a data breach or system outage, the matter is handled under the company's Incident Response process, and the outcome is fed back into the risk register to update the relevant risk's likelihood, impact, or treatment plan.

<!-- evidence:fa5a7cf96f8311bc2fbbc0ed source:word/document.xml#p00057 -->
10. Monitoring, Review, and Reporting

<!-- evidence:e75e79aef5c80fb6a435b027 source:word/document.xml#p00058 -->
The risk register and overall risk posture are reviewed at least annually by the Risk Owner together with relevant function leads. Ad hoc reviews are triggered by events such as a new product launch, a funding round, a security incident, or a material change in applicable law or regulation.

<!-- evidence:9ef5c8567cb4c19738951a59 source:word/document.xml#p00059 -->
A summary of the top risks, their status, and any material changes since the last review is shared with company leadership on a regular cadence, and with the board or investors where governance arrangements call for it.

<!-- evidence:bd2e02bd670919533bfe1b81 source:word/document.xml#p00062 -->
11. Integration with Other Policies

<!-- evidence:8f37605287dc7d3acef23137 source:word/document.xml#p00063 -->
This policy operates alongside, and is a foundation for, other company policies. Information security risks identified here inform the company's Information Security Policy and technical controls. Risks that materialize into actual events are handled under Incident Response and, where relevant, Business Continuity procedures. Vendor-related risks connect directly to vendor management and contracting practices.

<!-- evidence:b9ccca083c71d3c1a1057c7f source:word/document.xml#p00064 -->
Risks relating to personnel, such as those arising during recruitment, onboarding, or offboarding, are cross-referenced with the company's HR Policy, including the background verification requirements in the recruitment section and the access revocation steps in the offboarding checklist.

<!-- evidence:cd61b0faa2a323837721fbc6 source:word/document.xml#p00067 -->
12. Review and Continuous Improvement

<!-- evidence:b8af17a51c9e54d16b3eca52 source:word/document.xml#p00068 -->
This policy is reviewed at least once per year to ensure it remains aligned with the company's size, risk profile, and applicable legal and regulatory requirements. Reviews are also triggered by significant incidents, major organizational changes, or relevant regulatory updates.

<!-- evidence:51640297972ea100d6c54b3b source:word/document.xml#p00069 -->
Any revisions are subject to review and approval by the Policy Approver(s), after which updated versions are distributed to relevant personnel. Employees with risk-related responsibilities are notified of material changes and, where appropriate, asked to reacknowledge their understanding.

<!-- evidence:33bd377fdc55c306e4618128 source:word/document.xml#p00072 -->
13. Policy Acknowledgment

<!-- evidence:2c636f1843bd30616a450a25 source:word/document.xml#p00073 -->
All employees and contractors with risk-related responsibilities, including function leads and anyone handling customer data or critical systems, are required to review this policy and confirm their understanding through a documented acknowledgment process managed by HR or the Risk Owner.

<!-- evidence:5c5b76121dd2d7765162f4a6 source:word/document.xml#p00074 -->
Failure to identify, escalate, or address known risks in accordance with this policy may result in disciplinary action under the company's HR Policy, up to and including termination, and where applicable, legal consequences.
