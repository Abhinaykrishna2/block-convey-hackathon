1. Purpose and Scope

<!-- evidence:8ceeee0e9ed18b5389824497 source:word/document.xml#p00019 -->
This Information Security Policy defines how the company protects the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of the information it holds and processes. It applies to all information, data, cloud environments, endpoints, and SaaS applications owned, operated, or managed by the company, and to all founders, employees, interns, contractors, and third parties with access to those systems or data.

<!-- evidence:8d16f9a7df7fcf15d90033f3 source:word/document.xml#p00020 -->
The policy is designed to be proportionate to the size and stage of the organization, a small, cloud-native, remote-first team, rather than modeled on enterprise-scale frameworks. Controls are scoped to what the company implements and can evidence today, with planned improvements noted where relevant. It works alongside the Access Control, Data Classification, Cryptography, Vulnerability and Patch Management, Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery, Risk Management, Incident Management, and Human Resource policies.

<!-- evidence:ced6c1cc55702277c12ed5ec source:word/document.xml#p00021 -->
2. Roles and Responsibilities

<!-- evidence:5c4b516341642070d98c6e26 source:word/document.xml#p00022 -->
Given the company’s size, it does not maintain separate security, incident-response, disaster-recovery, or internal-audit teams. These functions are performed by the founders.

<!-- evidence:353f2d32a73f8dd83b321374 source:word/document.xml#p00023 -->
The Chief Executive Officer, acting as Chief Information Security Officer, holds overall accountability for information security, owns cloud infrastructure and IT, approves policies, and leads the annual risk assessment.

<!-- evidence:bca953ad7795d63542fb8e29 source:word/document.xml#p00024 -->
The Chief Technology Officer owns application code, deployments, and change management, is primarily responsible for access provisioning and deprovisioning, and holds standing production access.

<!-- evidence:5082e478ae029bfd87d2229d source:word/document.xml#p00025 -->
The Chief Business Officer / Chief Privacy Officer is responsible for all non-technical functions, including people operations, privacy, and compliance.

<!-- evidence:0f74cc1d7882fac0c9f8ce84 source:word/document.xml#p00026 -->
All personnel comply with policies, report suspected incidents, and complete security awareness training.

<!-- evidence:b9e62d3989adcf106972bc1a source:word/document.xml#p00027 -->
Third parties and vendors adhere to the security and confidentiality obligations in their contracts.

<!-- evidence:e4425ffd988486608b103157 source:word/document.xml#p00028 -->
Segregation of duties is applied where feasible; the company acknowledges that team size limits full separation of some duties.

<!-- evidence:33e68d3392f22cb0176360bc source:word/document.xml#p00029 -->
3. Risk Management

<!-- evidence:2c8b006fd0ddf6b490f726ca source:word/document.xml#p00030 -->
A risk assessment is conducted at least annually, led by the acting CISO, and is supplemented by third-party vulnerability assessment and penetration testing. Identified risks are tracked to remediation on a risk-based schedule. The overall approach is governed by the Risk Management Policy.

<!-- evidence:ce3d8abaf2875f63c8f4a963 source:word/document.xml#p00031 -->
4. Information Classification and Handling

<!-- evidence:3fee2b49d482764ecac0668f source:word/document.xml#p00032 -->
Information is classified as Public, Internal, Confidential, or Restricted. The Data Classification Policy is the single source of truth for these levels and for the handling, retention, and disposal requirements attached to each. Confidential and Restricted information is access-controlled and encrypted; Public information is managed for accuracy but is not access-restricted.

<!-- evidence:e4dbde9b7eb9e0e7a26517dd source:word/document.xml#p00033 -->
5. Access Control and Authentication

<!-- evidence:29069c9b30ef79c4f5954dd9 source:word/document.xml#p00034 -->
Access follows least privilege and is granted based on role. Multi-factor authentication is required across all core systems, including cloud infrastructure consoles, the identity and email provider, and the source-code platform. Production and non-production workloads run in separate cloud accounts. Detailed provisioning, review, and revocation controls are defined in the Access Control Policy.

<!-- evidence:eef1cec5f2d4601a7e7abcce source:word/document.xml#p00035 -->
6. Personnel Security

<!-- evidence:adcb0f398096387c8e242cda source:word/document.xml#p00036 -->
Background checks are conducted for all personnel through a third-party verification provider. Non-disclosure obligations are included in offer letters for all personnel, and any missing agreements are backfilled. Onboarding includes provisioning of least-privilege access and security awareness training. On offboarding, access is revoked promptly: revocation is requested through the company’s internal channel and executed by the designated administrator, with the Chief Technology Officer accountable for completion. Personnel controls are governed by the Human Resource Policy.

<!-- evidence:df8ed793c25266fa746da446 source:word/document.xml#p00037 -->
7. Security Awareness Training

<!-- evidence:b2189db7a10e755658ce4477 source:word/document.xml#p00038 -->
Security awareness training is delivered through the company’s own platform, at onboarding and on an ongoing, periodic basis. This program has recently been instituted and will continue on a regular cadence.

<!-- evidence:ba5a4b71346e64c7b1c41bb6 source:word/document.xml#p00039 -->
8. Network and Infrastructure Security

<!-- evidence:e9ac74b1f8c8c250a814e372 source:word/document.xml#p00040 -->
Network access is controlled using cloud-native firewalls configured to restrict inbound traffic. Production and non-production environments are separated at the cloud account level. Remote access to internal resources is being consolidated onto the company VPN; this rollout is in progress. The company operates no on-premises servers or data centers.

<!-- evidence:ce8d84a0ac04aa31ab102e8b source:word/document.xml#p00041 -->
9. Endpoint Security

<!-- evidence:eb5746b7eca1cf59e974f9f3 source:word/document.xml#p00042 -->
Endpoints used for work are kept current with operating-system security updates and built-in protections. Disk encryption is enabled where supported by the device. Devices accessing company systems must meet basic security requirements.

<!-- evidence:b8f7a9c7ce5d7845846e3d91 source:word/document.xml#p00043 -->
10. Cryptography

<!-- evidence:e0d4dbb70270f46d741a5e30 source:word/document.xml#p00044 -->
Sensitive data is encrypted at rest using AES-256 and in transit using TLS. Cryptographic standards and key handling are defined in the Cryptography Policy.

<!-- evidence:afd6c1afd743e93d5a4bebdd source:word/document.xml#p00045 -->
11. Logging and Monitoring

<!-- evidence:0d597b05bd67edff0805f3ae source:word/document.xml#p00046 -->
Cloud-native monitoring is used across the company’s cloud providers. Application logs are rotated on the host and shipped to object storage for retention. The cloud provider’s logging service retains logs for approximately one year, and object storage is retained on a long-term basis. The company does not currently operate a dedicated SIEM; centralized log analytics is a planned improvement.

<!-- evidence:a5a91b2638e4020519185658 source:word/document.xml#p00047 -->
12. Remote Work and Physical Security

<!-- evidence:35932006bf08864167d2d242 source:word/document.xml#p00048 -->
The workforce is remote-first. Encrypted collaboration and email are provided through the company’s productivity suite, and endpoints are kept updated. VPN access is being rolled out for internal resources. Production systems are hosted with major cloud providers, which provide physical and environmental security for their data centers; the company owns no data centers or server rooms. Physical office presence is limited to a co-working facility with card-based building access, and most staff work remotely.

<!-- evidence:43df6d497055af8d78b1d84f source:word/document.xml#p00049 -->
13. Change Management and Secure Development

<!-- evidence:067911935acdd3c7444039bd source:word/document.xml#p00050 -->
Source code is managed in a version-control platform, and changes require peer pull-request review before merge. Production changes require approval from the Chief Technology Officer, the Chief Business Officer / Chief Privacy Officer, or the Chief Executive Officer. Changes and technical decisions are documented in the company’s internal knowledge base. Production and non-production environments are separated, and developers follow secure coding practices to avoid common vulnerabilities.

<!-- evidence:27d4604beac779250f04bcbc source:word/document.xml#p00051 -->
14. Vulnerability and Patch Management

<!-- evidence:561c2a8d985784ef44eb4cd6 source:word/document.xml#p00052 -->
Vulnerabilities are identified through third-party vulnerability assessment and penetration testing, conducted at least annually, together with provider-native tooling. Patches are prioritized by severity and applied on a risk-based schedule. Full details are defined in the Vulnerability and Patch Management Policy.

<!-- evidence:f0a5e8dadbeeca585683290d source:word/document.xml#p00053 -->
15. Incident Response

<!-- evidence:dffda9bf4b722e6d30840b86 source:word/document.xml#p00054 -->
Suspected security incidents are reported through the company’s internal channels to the founders. The acting CISO and the Chief Technology Officer coordinate containment, remediation, and post-incident review. Given company size, there is no separate incident-response team; roles are assumed by the founders as needed. Detailed handling is defined in the Incident Management Policy.

<!-- evidence:b1a063c068ed3dd2871a8bdf source:word/document.xml#p00055 -->
16. Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery

<!-- evidence:8ba39bf9b485d402a2c6bb99 source:word/document.xml#p00056 -->
Production databases are backed up automatically on a daily basis, and zonal disaster-recovery capability exists. A disaster-recovery test is planned at least annually. Full procedures are defined in the Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Policy.

<!-- evidence:4f64f55766445372f755cee9 source:word/document.xml#p00057 -->
17. Third-Party and Vendor Management

<!-- evidence:9926b042912f9a2db9b8e48a source:word/document.xml#p00058 -->
Due diligence is performed before granting a vendor access to systems or data. Vendor access is limited to least privilege and reviewed periodically, and contracts include confidentiality and data-protection obligations. Vendor risk is governed by the Risk Management Policy.

<!-- evidence:97733870306902276f8ae8c2 source:word/document.xml#p00059 -->
18. Integration with Other Policies

<!-- evidence:25346db5e2930a3fcdeb1f57 source:word/document.xml#p00060 -->
This policy is the umbrella under which the company's other security policies sit, and it sets the direction those policies implement in detail. Identity and authorization requirements stated here are implemented by the Access Control Policy and, for credentials and secrets, by the Password and Secrets Policy. Requirements for protecting data are given effect by the Cryptography Policy, while the sensitivity levels that determine how strongly any given data set must be protected are defined by the Data Classification Policy, which is the single source of truth for those levels. The systems and devices to which all of these apply are recorded and controlled under the Asset Management Policy.

<!-- evidence:8504ba7b8b735daa6e7ce121 source:word/document.xml#p00061 -->
Risks identified under the Risk Management Policy inform the controls required here, and weaknesses found through the Vulnerability and Patch Management Policy are remediated on the basis that policy defines. Where a risk or weakness becomes an actual event, it is handled under the Incident Management Policy, and where it affects the availability of systems or data, under the Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Policy. Personnel obligations referred to throughout, including background verification, confidentiality agreements, onboarding, and offboarding, are set out in the Human Resource Policy, and expectations of conduct in the Code of Conduct Policy.

<!-- evidence:0e39f7e60f080fcb49db04b7 source:word/document.xml#p00062 -->
19. Compliance

<!-- evidence:66a67e50dbe7e17b87bef208 source:word/document.xml#p00063 -->
SOC 2 is the primary framework the company is pursuing. The company also observes applicable data-protection laws relevant to the customer data it processes. This policy is reviewed at least annually and updated as the company grows or its controls change.

<!-- evidence:78593025c29dbcefa49f536c source:word/document.xml#p00064 -->
20. Email and Phishing Protection

<!-- evidence:7a13f1cf90b650f68426f6c7 source:word/document.xml#p00065 -->
Email is provided through the company’s productivity suite with its built-in spam and phishing filtering enabled. Phishing awareness is included in security training. Simulated phishing exercises are not currently performed.

<!-- evidence:11bb7088b5b474840b8d8559 source:word/document.xml#p00066 -->
21. Policy Acknowledgment

<!-- evidence:1d28728b9f353443f8705cd5 source:word/document.xml#p00067 -->
All personnel are required to read, understand, and acknowledge this policy during onboarding and whenever significant updates are issued. Failure to comply may result in disciplinary action under the Human Resource Policy, up to and including termination.
