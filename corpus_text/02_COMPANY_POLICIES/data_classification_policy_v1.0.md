1. Purpose and Scope

<!-- evidence:28c3ec72f27255a408116b74 source:word/document.xml#p00019 -->
This Data Classification Policy ensures that the company’s information is consistently classified according to its sensitivity, value, and criticality, so that appropriate handling and protection measures are applied. It applies to all founders, employees, interns, contractors, and third parties who access, create, process, transmit, or store company information in any form.

<!-- evidence:691296b2ace42bbe02a4b1b8 source:word/document.xml#p00020 -->
This policy is the single source of truth for classification levels across the company, and the levels defined here are the levels referenced by the Information Security Policy, the Access Control Policy, the Cryptography Policy, and the Asset Management Policy. It also governs the retention and disposal of classified information.

<!-- evidence:63b6f62dcdaf0ea84c6f887f source:word/document.xml#p00021 -->
2. Roles and Responsibilities

<!-- evidence:9db8b6e9d00159483543c2eb source:word/document.xml#p00022 -->
The Chief Business Officer / Chief Privacy Officer owns this policy, owns privacy and compliance matters, and arbitrates classification questions that are not clear-cut.

<!-- evidence:1bce209c9e8ddf40c488df84 source:word/document.xml#p00023 -->
The Chief Executive Officer, acting as Chief Information Security Officer, ensures that technical controls enforce the handling requirements attached to each classification level.

<!-- evidence:8d59edab5723ea5cc3a6fc99 source:word/document.xml#p00024 -->
The Chief Technology Officer classifies product and engineering data, including source code and customer data held in the product, and ensures classification is reflected in system configuration.

<!-- evidence:0c0ed8b709b10f1465abae24 source:word/document.xml#p00025 -->
Data owners, being the role accountable for a given data set, classify data on creation, reclassify it when circumstances change, and authorize access consistent with its classification.

<!-- evidence:cff5e88ac6ee31d724f86fba source:word/document.xml#p00026 -->
All personnel handle information according to its classification and raise any classification uncertainty to the data owner.

<!-- evidence:712fd16ffe7d1f8fe1a59af1 source:word/document.xml#p00027 -->
3. Classification Levels

<!-- evidence:7d7609ab0d13c27a6f60a167 source:word/document.xml#p00028 -->
The company uses four classification levels, in increasing order of sensitivity:

<!-- evidence:3dc5f7f4276b69931c0a7d1b source:word/document.xml#p00029 -->
Public: information authorized for release without restriction, where disclosure causes no harm.

<!-- evidence:a2c610741716e5d9006376fc source:word/document.xml#p00030 -->
Internal: information intended for use within the company and its authorized parties, where disclosure would cause minimal risk.

<!-- evidence:1a16cef90717f7ef0b1634ac source:word/document.xml#p00031 -->
Confidential: information where disclosure could cause moderate to high reputational, operational, financial, or legal harm.

<!-- evidence:4cf769a377964155dc81a502 source:word/document.xml#p00032 -->
Restricted: the company’s most sensitive information, where unauthorized disclosure would have severe legal, financial, or operational consequences.

<!-- evidence:843af4130c8097eeae22c408 source:word/document.xml#p00033 -->
Where a data set could reasonably fall into more than one level, the higher level applies. Appendix A provides a decision matrix with worked examples.

<!-- evidence:845f06ff85f0c8c7b50844b3 source:word/document.xml#p00034 -->
4. Criteria for Assigning a Level

<!-- evidence:5f01b563dedef8c66feb1dd8 source:word/document.xml#p00035 -->
Classification is assigned based on legal, regulatory, and contractual obligations; the potential financial, operational, reputational, or legal impact of unauthorized disclosure; intellectual property value and competitive advantage; and privacy implications where the data includes personal information or customer data.

<!-- evidence:47bc5c9cbe6766451c0eb5d1 source:word/document.xml#p00036 -->
5. Labeling

<!-- evidence:f9ab80c3de237592607d9ac9 source:word/document.xml#p00037 -->
Documents carry their classification in the document itself, in a header, footer, or document-control block, in the manner used by the company’s policy documents. System and repository configuration reflects classification through the access restrictions applied to it.

<!-- evidence:7240eca183bc48af536ceff8 source:word/document.xml#p00038 -->
The company does not require classification markings in email subject lines, physical labels on removable media, or verbal announcements of classification. These controls are not proportionate to a cloud-native, remote-first operating model in which information is held in access-controlled systems rather than circulated as physical media, and requiring them would create obligations the company could not consistently evidence. Removable media is not an approved storage location for Confidential or Restricted data.

<!-- evidence:5d2883038901cbf26763805e source:word/document.xml#p00039 -->
6. Access by Classification

<!-- evidence:e6edb4e57ef2fdc8273e3054 source:word/document.xml#p00040 -->
Access is granted according to classification and least privilege, enforced as set out in the Access Control Policy:

<!-- evidence:a3b3d7378fac553cb44e89dc source:word/document.xml#p00041 -->
Public: no access restrictions.

<!-- evidence:e3c6b9d69b42d718c207a5b2 source:word/document.xml#p00042 -->
Internal: available to personnel; not for external disclosure.

<!-- evidence:1101f7eda5a295ae94b374ad source:word/document.xml#p00043 -->
Confidential: limited to those with a legitimate need to know.

<!-- evidence:a9721edc6546fe06dd0fc689 source:word/document.xml#p00044 -->
Restricted: limited to specifically authorized roles, with multi-factor authentication and access logging.

<!-- evidence:4424f8337a2d5cbaf7948d26 source:word/document.xml#p00045 -->
7. Storage, Transmission, and Handling

<!-- evidence:3f099e07fec281a5a5d82cd6 source:word/document.xml#p00046 -->
Confidential and Restricted data is stored only in approved, access-controlled systems and is encrypted at rest using AES-256. Confidential and Restricted data is transmitted only over encrypted channels using TLS, as defined in the Cryptography Policy. Customer data used outside production is anonymized or protected to the same standard as production data. Confidential and Restricted data must not be stored in unapproved locations, including personal storage accounts, unmanaged local copies, or removable media.

<!-- evidence:035198f4a88bbd6ac8e04980 source:word/document.xml#p00047 -->
8. Retention

<!-- evidence:8a99a7aa6e16723ff03cbf68 source:word/document.xml#p00048 -->
Information is retained only as long as necessary for the business, legal, contractual, or regulatory purpose for which it is held. Retention for each data set is the longer of the period required by contract with a customer or partner, the period required by applicable law or regulation, and the period necessary for the company’s legitimate business purpose.

<!-- evidence:c876f75ddc017bdcecbd0bb0 source:word/document.xml#p00049 -->
The company does not set fixed multi-year retention periods that it cannot yet evidence. Because the company was incorporated recently, retained records extend from inception, and the company does not represent that records predate its incorporation. Retention periods are reassessed at least annually as the company matures and as customer and regulatory obligations require, and any retention term committed to a customer contractually takes precedence over the general position in this section.

<!-- evidence:c079c8c3072c5e85cfc74348 source:word/document.xml#p00050 -->
Log and audit-record retention follows the arrangements described in the Access Control Policy and the Cryptography Policy, under which the cloud provider’s logging service retains logs for approximately one year and logs shipped to object storage are retained on a long-term basis.

<!-- evidence:238a9aaca6093d6498b0116e source:word/document.xml#p00051 -->
9. Disposal

<!-- evidence:c1d145028d5858d5c6c4f661 source:word/document.xml#p00052 -->
Data no longer required is securely deleted. Cloud-hosted data is deleted using the cloud provider’s deletion mechanisms, supported by cryptographic erasure of the associated keys as described in the Cryptography Policy. Devices are wiped before reissue or disposal, and company data is removed from personally owned devices at departure, as described in the Asset Management Policy. Deletion carried out in response to a customer or data-subject request is performed within the timeframe required by the applicable contract or law.

<!-- evidence:971262bd47662ab5e58ca529 source:word/document.xml#p00053 -->
10. Third Parties

<!-- evidence:ac8745afbcf5115e870d16ac source:word/document.xml#p00054 -->
Third parties who access, process, or store the company’s classified data must be bound by confidentiality obligations, must handle the data in line with its classification, and must report any incident affecting it promptly. Vendor due diligence and tiering are carried out under the Risk Management Policy, and vendor access is governed by the Access Control Policy.

<!-- evidence:a913984dbad6d264e5a5cbf5 source:word/document.xml#p00055 -->
11. Training

<!-- evidence:2576aef5136a8f79d20aa028 source:word/document.xml#p00056 -->
Classification and handling requirements are covered at onboarding and in ongoing security awareness training delivered through the company’s platform, as described in the Information Security Policy and the Human Resource Policy.

<!-- evidence:a03d97bb8f82913460934bd6 source:word/document.xml#p00057 -->
12. Monitoring and Review

<!-- evidence:6efaf311c5ee363d337117c1 source:word/document.xml#p00058 -->
Access to classified data is reviewed at least annually as part of the access review described in the Access Control Policy, and classification is revisited when a data set’s use or sensitivity materially changes. Findings that indicate a control weakness are remediated and, where material, recorded in the risk register maintained under the Risk Management Policy.

<!-- evidence:eca0b95087300255f6cdc5e9 source:word/document.xml#p00059 -->
13. Integration with Other Policies

<!-- evidence:334e2096ee8f044865a687c0 source:word/document.xml#p00060 -->
This policy is the single source of truth for the company's classification levels, and it is therefore a dependency of most other security policies rather than merely a companion to them. The levels defined here determine the strength of access restriction applied under the Access Control Policy, whether and how data must be encrypted under the Cryptography Policy, the protection required of the systems and devices recorded under the Asset Management Policy, and the severity assigned to a weakness under the Vulnerability and Patch Management Policy.

<!-- evidence:addb1f0a512885f964aaeac8 source:word/document.xml#p00061 -->
Retention and disposal requirements set out here govern how long information is held and how it is destroyed, including the data restored or retained under the Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Policy. The sensitivity of data affected by an event informs its handling under the Incident Management Policy, and risks to classified information are recorded and treated under the Risk Management Policy. Obligations placed on personnel to handle information according to its classification are supported by the Human Resource Policy and the Code of Conduct Policy, and the overall direction for this policy is set by the Information Security Policy.

<!-- evidence:1413018885cc94fdebc00e7f source:word/document.xml#p00062 -->
14. Compliance and Review

<!-- evidence:5729a32f9ff7635ad056983f source:word/document.xml#p00063 -->
This policy supports the company’s SOC 2 objectives and the data-protection obligations applicable to the customer data the company processes. It is reviewed at least annually, and sooner following a significant incident, a change in regulatory requirements, or a material change to the business.

Internal: intended for personnel and authorized parties; minimal risk if disclosed.

<!-- evidence:01c756d5f71e14ba37da4ab6 source:word/document.xml#p00179 -->
Confidential: moderate to high risk if disclosed; can cause reputational, operational, or financial harm.

<!-- evidence:f1e7603b084f99c4070b574b source:word/document.xml#p00180 -->
