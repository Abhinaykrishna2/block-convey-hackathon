1. Purpose and Scope

<!-- evidence:df55fa6acdecfd3a60b7356d source:word/document.xml#p00019 -->
This Asset Management Policy establishes how the company identifies, records, protects, maintains, and disposes of the assets used to run its business and deliver its product. It applies to all founders, employees, interns, contractors, and third parties who use or manage company assets.

<!-- evidence:2c0238707ff826c857eee187 source:word/document.xml#p00020 -->
In scope are endpoint devices used for company work, cloud infrastructure resources, software-as-a-service subscriptions and applications, source code and data, and the credentials that grant access to them. The company operates no on-premises servers, data centers, or server rooms; physical and environmental controls for production infrastructure are provided by the cloud providers. This policy works alongside the Information Security Policy, the Access Control Policy, the Data Classification Policy, the Cryptography Policy, and the Vulnerability and Patch Management Policy.

<!-- evidence:d72c6bb6b3c7d46c760d003a source:word/document.xml#p00021 -->
2. Roles and Responsibilities

<!-- evidence:36e17c960d60fef909e547de source:word/document.xml#p00022 -->
The Chief Executive Officer, acting as Chief Information Security Officer, owns this policy, owns cloud infrastructure and IT assets, and leads the annual asset review.

<!-- evidence:01e6565b955df62f2ed207b2 source:word/document.xml#p00023 -->
The Chief Technology Officer owns source code, deployment tooling, and production resources, and ensures security configuration is applied to assets before use.

<!-- evidence:5126512b4f0364ccafee63a8 source:word/document.xml#p00024 -->
The Chief Business Officer / Chief Privacy Officer owns non-technical assets, including business and people-operations subscriptions, and coordinates device issue and return with onboarding and offboarding.

<!-- evidence:2ff54a2bb5403f59aeb7d4b4 source:word/document.xml#p00025 -->
All personnel use company assets in line with this policy, protect the assets in their possession, and report loss, theft, or malfunction promptly.

<!-- evidence:d322daeff3ac26fa4b79142c source:word/document.xml#p00026 -->
3. Asset Inventory

<!-- evidence:89d671cb02906f0aa466db8a source:word/document.xml#p00027 -->
The company maintains its asset inventory on its internal compliance platform, which serves as the single record of assets in use.

<!-- evidence:8dc703bffd89e2629097d766 source:word/document.xml#p00028 -->
Endpoint devices used for company work are recorded on the internal compliance platform, including the assigned holder and whether the device is company-issued or personally owned.

<!-- evidence:fe9d024226962c071133b690 source:word/document.xml#p00029 -->
Cloud infrastructure resources are collected automatically on a daily basis and maintained on the internal compliance platform, so the infrastructure inventory reflects the current state of the environment rather than a periodic manual snapshot.

<!-- evidence:b8eaa0fd7e29e9f6aff16a51 source:word/document.xml#p00030 -->
Software-as-a-service subscriptions and applications holding company or customer data are recorded, together with their business owner.

<!-- evidence:b0de532338dc56b16239e22d source:word/document.xml#p00031 -->
Inventory records identify the asset, its owner or assigned holder, and its criticality. The inventory is reviewed at least annually alongside the access review described in the Access Control Policy, and is updated on issue, return, or decommissioning of an asset.

<!-- evidence:f9661e704e71832bf0961b23 source:word/document.xml#p00032 -->
4. Asset Classification

<!-- evidence:59dd23afdd0de4e95633405b source:word/document.xml#p00033 -->
Assets are classified according to the sensitivity of the data they hold or access, using the classification levels defined in the Data Classification Policy. Assets that store or process Confidential or Restricted data are subject to the strongest protection requirements, including encryption and restricted access.

<!-- evidence:1002396c91dbe68dc1f908d0 source:word/document.xml#p00034 -->
5. Endpoint Security

<!-- evidence:db28cd32bb17bc31b5feb6b7 source:word/document.xml#p00035 -->
Both company-issued and personally owned devices may be used for company work. Company-issued devices are the default for personnel in roles that require them. Security requirements attach to the device and the data on it rather than to its ownership, and apply equally in both cases.

<!-- evidence:b51f818edf4b1b785cbb2fab source:word/document.xml#p00036 -->
Every device used for company work must have full-disk encryption enabled, must run a supported operating system kept current with security updates, must use the operating system’s native malware protection and firewall, and must be protected by a screen lock and strong authentication. Devices are hardened using the operating system’s native security configuration. The company does not deploy a separate commercial endpoint detection and response product; it relies on operating-system native protections, which are verified as part of the annual asset review.

<!-- evidence:40441dfac3bfa927c8decc2c source:word/document.xml#p00037 -->
Loss or theft of any device used for company work must be reported immediately and is handled under the Incident Management Policy, including revocation of the credentials held on that device.

<!-- evidence:86d41cee782ac807a94ea276 source:word/document.xml#p00038 -->
6. Cloud and Production Assets

<!-- evidence:d70127b677994cc7836cabea source:word/document.xml#p00039 -->
Production and non-production workloads run in separate cloud accounts. Network access to production resources is controlled using cloud-native firewalls configured to restrict inbound traffic. Production data stores are encrypted at rest, and customer data used outside production is anonymized or protected to the same standard as production data, as set out in the Cryptography Policy and the Data Classification Policy.

<!-- evidence:be084e1244075ba104d4b952 source:word/document.xml#p00040 -->
Access to production resources is restricted and logged, as defined in the Access Control Policy.

<!-- evidence:b1c2c75340957d7a50becf72 source:word/document.xml#p00041 -->
7. Asset Lifecycle

<!-- evidence:c2470f6a4d965637476d0d72 source:word/document.xml#p00042 -->
Acquisition and Deployment

<!-- evidence:1653e42fcc37529d1da5c05c source:word/document.xml#p00043 -->
Assets are procured through authorized channels and recorded on the internal compliance platform before or at issue. Security configuration, including disk encryption, operating-system updates, and access provisioning on a least-privilege basis, is applied before an asset is used for company work.

<!-- evidence:daba71889f8e297718d5e067 source:word/document.xml#p00044 -->
Maintenance

<!-- evidence:19afebec0176c8d53b64396b source:word/document.xml#p00045 -->
Assets are kept on supported software versions and patched in line with the Vulnerability and Patch Management Policy. Inventory records are updated as assets are issued, reassigned, or retired.

<!-- evidence:ed03901eabf2d32d4f2ad31e source:word/document.xml#p00046 -->
Return and Disposal

<!-- evidence:dc9d435fed02f5357330a271 source:word/document.xml#p00047 -->
Company-issued devices are collected before a person’s departure and are securely wiped before reissue or disposal. Where a personally owned device has been used for company work, company data is removed and access is revoked at departure. Device return and data removal are carried out as part of the offboarding process defined in the Human Resource Policy, alongside the access revocation described in the Access Control Policy, and the inventory record is updated to reflect the outcome.

<!-- evidence:5a5474099dacf9be64f55269 source:word/document.xml#p00048 -->
Cloud storage and infrastructure are decommissioned using the cloud provider’s deletion mechanisms, with cryptographic erasure of associated keys as described in the Cryptography Policy. Physical destruction of media in the cloud environment is the responsibility of the cloud provider under its own certified processes.

<!-- evidence:2fccc309156723afa6e7d6ad source:word/document.xml#p00049 -->
8. Acceptable Use

<!-- evidence:ac314bc7a7678dfe5051b77b source:word/document.xml#p00050 -->
Company assets are provided for business use. Personnel must not install unauthorized software that introduces security risk, must not disable required security controls such as disk encryption or operating-system updates, and must not store company data outside approved systems. Conduct expectations are set out in the Code of Conduct Policy.

<!-- evidence:9ee8aac85244426fdd565785 source:word/document.xml#p00051 -->
9. Monitoring and Review

<!-- evidence:6542e3acb2925c2c1d2d13b9 source:word/document.xml#p00052 -->
Cloud-native monitoring and logging are used across company infrastructure, as described in the Information Security Policy. Logs and alerts are reviewed for anomalies, and findings that indicate a control weakness are recorded and remediated, and where material are added to the risk register maintained under the Risk Management Policy.

<!-- evidence:e4346e53960980204cb96561 source:word/document.xml#p00053 -->
10. Integration with Other Policies

<!-- evidence:ffd22e4db27035f06d83912e source:word/document.xml#p00054 -->
This policy establishes what the company owns and operates, which is the foundation the other security policies act upon. An asset cannot be protected, patched, or recovered unless it is known, so the inventory maintained here is what gives scope to the Access Control Policy, the Vulnerability and Patch Management Policy, and the Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Policy.

<!-- evidence:3607da4ba160ed281fedda7e source:word/document.xml#p00055 -->
The sensitivity of the data an asset holds is determined by the Data Classification Policy, and that classification drives the protection this policy requires of it, with encryption performed as set out in the Cryptography Policy. Access to assets is granted and revoked under the Access Control Policy, and device issue and return are carried out as part of the onboarding and offboarding processes in the Human Resource Policy. Loss or theft of an asset is handled under the Incident Management Policy, and risks arising from the asset estate are recorded under the Risk Management Policy. Expectations for the use of company assets are set out in the Code of Conduct Policy, and the overall direction for this policy is set by the Information Security Policy.

<!-- evidence:09369bba09a946807226477d source:word/document.xml#p00056 -->
11. Compliance and Review

<!-- evidence:007977da011185d77bd97b9d source:word/document.xml#p00057 -->
Asset management practices support the company’s SOC 2 objectives. This policy is reviewed at least annually and updated as the company grows or its controls change.
